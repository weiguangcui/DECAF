Abstract: Euclid is a cosmology survey mission designed to constrain the properties of dark energy and dark matter. Its wide survey will cover 15,000 deg² (~1/3 of the sky), complemented by three deep fields totaling ~10% of observing time. Fornax is one of these deep fields, covering 10 deg².

To maximise the science return of the Euclid Fornax deep field, 1000 h of XMM-Newton time was allocated in 2023 to observe it at a uniform depth of 40 ks. The programe will complete in 2027, with an expected yield of several thousand AGN and several hundred galaxy clusters. Central to the XMM programe is the detection of the faint cluster population below the detection threshold of the Euclid wide survey, essential for Euclid's cosmological constraints from cluster number counts. I will present the FornaX survey: its scientific goals, the current status of XMM-Newton observations, and our first results from a joint XMM-Newton simulation programe.

Speaker Bio: Iván Muñoz Rodríguez is a postdoctoral researcher at CEA Paris-Saclay, where he works on X-ray surveys of galaxy clusters and Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) in the FornaX Euclid–XMM deep field. He did his PhD (2020–2024) as a Marie Skłodowska-Curie fellow in the BiD4BESt network, between the National Observatory of Athens and the University of Southampton. His research covers supermassive black hole growth, how environment affects AGN activity, and cluster detection and selection functions, combining observations, simulations and statistical modelling.
